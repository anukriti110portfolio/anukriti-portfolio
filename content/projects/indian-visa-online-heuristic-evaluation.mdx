<Section title="Chapter 1 · The first welcome">

<Lead>Before a foreigner sees India, they see indianvisaonline.gov.in.</Lead>

For most visitors, the first channel into India is not an airport or a hotel. It is this website. The first site foreigners meet on their way to India is Indian Visa Online, so I decided to audit it: if this is the front door of the country, how does it feel to walk through it?

<Section title="Chapter 6 · First redesign: ask before you send">

<Lead>Help people choose a route before they have to guess.</Lead>

The home page is too cluttered for a visitor to know where to go. Instead of navigating through multiple funnels, the website directly asks users what they need and guides them to where to go next: a guided entry that asks why you are coming and for how long, then recommends a path.

<Section title="Chapter 7 · The second wall">

<Lead>Even after choosing right, the "Apply" button leads to a page of rules.</Lead>

Step 2 of the same trip: clicking the e-Tourist Visa CTA opens an instruction page, not the form. You have to scroll to find the actual application.

<Facts
  cols={2}
  items={[
    { "title": "Step 2 · e-tourist visa CTA bait", "text": "The Apply CTA opens an instruction page, not the form; you have to scroll to find the actual application." },
    { "title": "What it costs the user", "text": "The user thinks they clicked \"apply\" but gets hit with dense rules they are not ready to read. That is friction." },
    { "title": "Severity (guess)", "text": "Medium" },
    { "title": "Opportunity", "text": "Unclutter the information architecture." }
  ]}
/>

<Spread
  cols={1}

It is mandatory for the user to know the instructions before applying for a tourist visa. But users usually scan rather than read, so by default they click the blue e-Tourist Visa button. That is an expectation mismatch, and the information architecture of this screen can be better.

</Section>

<Section title="Chapter 8 · What comes next">

<Lead>Declutter and structure the e-Visa screen the way the home page was.</Lead>

The same principle that reshaped the home page applies here: show the next decision first, and keep the rules close but out of the way. The redesign of the e-Visa screen is the next step in this case study.
