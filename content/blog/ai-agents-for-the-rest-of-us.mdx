<Section title="In short">

An AI agent is an assistant that does not only answer your question, but also does the work. You give it a goal, and it plans the steps, uses tools, checks the result and tries again if something fails. This is how I understood it as a designer with no engineering background, and how I now use it.

</Section>

<Section title="What is an AI agent?">

Most of us first met AI as a chat box. You type a question, it types an answer, and then you go and do the work yourself.

An agent closes that gap. Think of the difference between asking a friend for directions and asking a driver to take you there.

<Facts
  cols={2}
  items={[
    { title: "A chatbot", bullets: ["Answers one question at a time", "Waits for you to do the next step", "Gives you text to copy and use"] },
    { title: "An agent", bullets: ["Takes a goal, not just a question", "Breaks it into steps and does them", "Looks at the result and fixes its own mistakes"] },
  ]}
/>

</Section>

<Section title="How it works, without the jargon">

Almost every agent follows the same simple loop, which is also how a person works on a task.

<Flow items={["Goal", "Plan", "Act with tools", "Check the result", "Adjust and repeat"]} />

The tools are the part that surprised me. An agent can open files, write and run code, search the web or use other apps, so that it does the task in front of you, not just talks about it. "Agentic AI" is simply the name for AI systems that work this way.

</Section>

<Section title="How I learned">

I did not learn it from a course. I learned by using an agent to build the website you are reading.

I am a designer, and this portfolio was my first serious front-end build. I described what I wanted in plain words: add my IIT Guwahati projects to the playground, remove a section, update my links. The agent read the project, changed the files, ran the site to check it and showed me screenshots. When something broke, it read the error and fixed it.

Four things I learned along the way:

<Steps
  items={[
    { title: "Be specific about the goal", text: "Vague requests gave vague results. \"Make it look good\" worked far less well than \"keep my text and images as they are, but present them in a minimalist layout\"." },
    { title: "Give it the real material", text: "It did its best work when I attached my actual PDFs and files instead of describing them." },
    { title: "Work in small steps and look at every result", text: "I treated each change like a design review. I looked, said what felt off, and it adjusted." },
    { title: "I stay the decision maker", text: "It can build quickly, but taste, order, wording and what is true about my work are still mine to check." },
  ]}
/>

</Section>

<Section title="How I use it">

For me, it is a patient collaborator that handles the parts I cannot do yet.

- It helps me turn project reports into web pages while keeping my words and images.
- It updates the site when I want to change a section, a link or an order.
- It explains what it did, which is slowly teaching me how my own site works.

</Section>

<Section title="Where to be careful">

- **It can be confidently wrong.** Check facts, numbers and anything written about your work.
- **It does what you ask, not what you meant.** Review the result before it goes live.
- **Mind what you share.** Do not hand over private or confidential client material to tools you do not understand.
- **Your taste is the point.** An agent speeds up making, and deciding what is worth making is still the designer's job.

</Section>

<Section title="Try it yourself">

Pick one small, real task, such as turning a PDF into a simple page. Describe the goal clearly, give it your files and review each step. You will learn more in an afternoon than from reading about it.

</Section>
