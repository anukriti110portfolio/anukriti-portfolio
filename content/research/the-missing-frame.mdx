## The brief

Design an app that helps ordinary people pose and click great photos of
themselves or of others. The usual suspects were poor posing, incorrect framing
and camera angles, unfavourable lighting, distracting backgrounds, and a lack
of photography knowledge. This study asks one question before proposing any
solution: are people really struggling because they don't know how to take a
photograph?

## What the evidence points to

Four possible explanations sit behind the same disappointing photograph:
knowledge, execution, confidence and perception. Mapping the causal chains and
interviewing three people showed a repeated pattern. The subject imagines a
photograph and the photographer cannot reliably reproduce it. The camera gives
its judgement after capture, not during it.

## The research gap

The camera understands the frame, but does it understand the relationship
behind the frame? Three gaps stand out: the subject cannot always turn a mental
image into clear instructions, the photographer may not understand the desired
pose, angle or composition, and there is no shared, continuous reference that
shows what needs correcting before the shutter is pressed.

## Problem statement

People struggle to achieve their intended photographs when a non-expert
photographer is involved, because the subject's visual intention is not
effectively translated into actionable framing, positioning and capture
decisions.

## Gaps a designer can work on

**Open challenge:** explore an experience that bridges the communication and
execution gap between a subject and a non-expert photographer. The primary
interaction to investigate runs from subject intention, to a shared visual
reference, to photographer guidance, to capture, to feedback.
